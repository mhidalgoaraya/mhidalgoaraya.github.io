About

I am a PhD researcher at Eindhoven University of Technology, in BIASlab, the Bayesian intelligent autonomous systems group. I build probabilistic models: models that combine what engineers already know about a system with what the data shows, and that report how certain they are.

My work so far covers robust tracking that keeps working when sensors misbehave, speech enhancement that runs on a hearing-aid-class processor, and identification of machines from their physics. The common thread is a way of computing called message passing: small, local calculations that stream in real time and stay explainable.

This blog collects case studies written for people who decide whether a technology is worth adopting: what problem it solves, what it costs to run, what it cannot do, and a demo to try.

Experience

  • PhD candidate, Eindhoven University of Technology, BIASlab (completion expected 2027). Tempered message passing, robust Kalman filtering, probabilistic speech enhancement for hearing aids.
  • Clinical Data & AI Scientist, Philips Research, Eindhoven (2021 onwards). Signal processing and deep learning for remote patient monitoring: non-invasive blood pressure from wearables, fetal heart rate monitoring from an ECG patch. Translating results into decisions for senior stakeholders.
  • Biomedical R&D Engineer, imec (2018–2021). Led the dry-electrode research programme; real-time pipelines for wearable ECG, EEG, EMG and bio-impedance sensors.
  • Research Scholar, Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, Chicago (2017–2018). Wearable-sensor clinical study to predict recovery after stroke.
  • R&D Engineer, Cobybsa, Costa Rica (2014–2016). Led a team of ten delivering turnkey manufacturing lines for multinationals including Coca-Cola and Unilever: plant redesign, conveyors, powder mixers and sanitary pipelines, from mechanical design and budget to installation.
