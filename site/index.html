Models that know how sure they are.

Case studies in building small, explainable models for machines and devices: how they work, what they cost to run, and what they can and cannot do. Each one comes with a demo you can try.

Why this matters

Industry has run many AI pilots and deployed few of them. In manufacturing and medical technology the blocker is rarely accuracy. It is trust. Errors are expensive, behaviour has to be explained to a plant manager, an auditor or a regulator, and the system has to keep working when conditions drift.

The models in these case studies are built for that situation. They start from what engineers already know about a system, state their assumptions in plain terms, report how sure they are, and run on the hardware that is already there.

Two layers

  • Estimation and monitoring. Tracking the state of a machine, a process or a patient, identifying how a system behaves, and flagging faults, each with an honest error bar.
  • An agent that reduces uncertainty. On top of a trusted model: deciding what to measure next, when to recalibrate, and when to ask an operator, so that its uncertainty about the physical system goes down within stated limits. This is a different kind of agent from a chatbot. It acts on a model of your system, not on plausible text.
