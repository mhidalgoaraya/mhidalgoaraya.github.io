A water rig whose twin knows when the sensor lies

A pump lifts water into an upper tank, which drains into a lower tank, which drains back to the reservoir. Only the lower tank has a level sensor. The twin is a physics model of this rig. It reads the sensor, estimates the upper tank it cannot see, forecasts the next five minutes with an honest uncertainty band, and turns down its trust in readings that make no physical sense.

How hard the pump pushes. The twin was only tested between 0.4 and 6.5 V, so it clamps the pump to that range.

What is wrong with the plant?

The twin also tracks four physical parameters, slowly, from the same readings. Break the simulated rig and watch.

A steady pump hides most faults. Automatic drive (left) moves the pump and makes them visible sooner; an upper-outlet clog needs the upper sensor.

Physics you can read

Water flows out of each tank like out of a bottle (Torricelli's law), plus small losses. The upper tank overflows at its rim and about three quarters of the spill lands in the lower tank; the lower tank overflows at its own rim, just above the sensor's 10 V limit. A small learned correction covers what the physics misses.

A sensor model with doubts

Every reading gets a trust weight. Readings that agree with the physics get full weight; a reading that jumps several volts in four seconds gets almost none, so one bad sample cannot drag the estimate. Readings stuck at 10 V are treated as "at least 10 V", not as exactly 10.

Honest uncertainty

The shaded band is where the twin expects 95 % of readings to fall. On the real test record it is off by 0.20 V on average (RMSE) and the band holds 94 % of the readings, using only the pump voltage after the first 200 seconds.
