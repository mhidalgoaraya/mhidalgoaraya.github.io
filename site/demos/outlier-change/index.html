Glitch or real change? Ask which trust dropped

A delivery robot drives down a three-lane road, and a GPS reports which way it has drifted sideways. Sometimes the GPS glitches for one reading. Sometimes the robot really changes lane. On the first odd reading the two look exactly the same. The tracker keeps two trust weights, one for the sensor and one for its own idea of how the robot moves, and lets the next reading decide which one to lower.

Both surprises are 3 m, so the first odd reading looks the same either way. A glitch is gone at the next reading; a lane change stays. The two-weight tracker waits one reading (a quarter of a second) before it decides.

Well-log: 675 readings of a probe lowered into a borehole (Ó Ruanaidh and Fitzgerald, 1996). The rock changes in layers, and the record also holds isolated spikes. Same three trackers, settings tuned on the first half as in the paper; each decides three readings later.

Two weights, two suspects

Every reading enters the tracker through two doors: "the sensor says" and "robots move smoothly". Each door has a trust weight that starts at 100 %. A surprising reading must lower one of them. Lower the sensor's weight and the estimate holds; lower the motion model's weight and the estimate jumps to follow.

The next reading decides

Sensor glitch: the measurement's weight drops, the estimate holds. Real change: the motion model's weight drops, the estimate follows. One odd reading cannot tell the two apart; the reading after it can, because a glitch does not repeat and a lane change does. So the tracker decides one reading late.

Same scale, any units

The weights are "units-invariant": measure in metres or millimetres and they come out the same. With only the sensor weight, this tracker is the classic Student-t filter; with neither, it is the ordinary Kalman filter, which trusts every reading and is dragged by every glitch.

Local-level model, Q/R = 0.02 (live). Kalman filter; Student-t filter (sensor weight only, prior CV 0.5); two-weight tempered message passing (TMP, sensor CV 0.35, motion CV 2, one-reading window, 50 iterations).
