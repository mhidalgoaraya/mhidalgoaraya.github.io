Fault monitoring · SKAB water-circulation test rig · replay of the model's analysis

A test rig whose monitor says what broke

A pump circulates water from a tank through a loop of pipes. Eight sensors report once a second. Engineers broke the rig on purpose: they closed valves, let water leak, unbalanced the rotor. A small model of the healthy rig, learned from normal running only, watches every reading. When the rig leaves its healthy band, the monitor writes down which sensor, what kind of change, and how large.

    A model of the healthy rig

    About 140 numbers describe how each sensor normally wobbles and how sensors move together: motor current with voltage, the two vibration sensors with each other. They were learned from normal running only; the model has never seen a fault. The violet band is where it expects each reading, one second ahead.

    A short list of explanations

    Every second the monitor weighs "all normal" against a few named changes: a sensor jumped, started drifting, became noisier or calmer, or several jumped together. The timeline shows how much each explanation is winning. When "something changed" passes 50 %, it raises an alarm and writes the winner as a sentence.

    Cautious by design

    Its confidence is tempered: tuned on normal data so that it raises about one false alarm per 20 minutes of healthy running. On SKAB's 34 faults it scores 42.5 on the benchmark's own scale, above every published entry, deep networks included. It still misses small valve changes, and its first message names the expected sensor in about two thirds of the faults.
